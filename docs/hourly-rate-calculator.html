This is what you actually want to see in your bank account each year, after tax. It is not what you invoice: that number, your gross income, is higher once tax, super and expenses are added. A graduate designer in Melbourne might aim for $45–55k to start; a midweight designer $70–90k.
Superannuation is compulsory retirement savings. As a freelancer nobody pays it for you, so you build it into your rate and set it aside yourself. The legislated rate is 12% of gross income. Leave it out and you are working for less than your headline rate every year, and arriving at retirement with nothing saved.

Time and lifestyle

Leave and time off
Freelancers get no paid leave. Every week you are not working is a week you are not earning, so this setting cuts your available hours and pushes your rate up to compensate. Standard (4 weeks) is the bare minimum. Balanced (6 weeks) is realistic once you count public holidays and the odd sick day. Underestimating this is one of the most common mistakes new freelancers make.

Full time: fixed costs spread across the most hours, so the rate is lowest
Working part time does not reduce your fixed costs. Software, insurance, your desk and your equipment cost the same whether you work two days or five, so those costs have to be recovered across fewer hours. Your income target scales down at the same time, which is why the rate moves less than you might expect.
Not every hour at your desk earns money. Replying to emails, writing quotes, chasing invoices, updating your portfolio and keeping your skills current all take real time. For a freelancer starting out, 20–25% is realistic. Underestimating this is a fast way to underprice your work.

Assume you take a coworking desk at least two days a week. It is not an indulgence: it gives you somewhere credible to meet a client, it separates work from home so you can stop at the end of the day, and most early freelance work arrives through people you actually meet. Working alone from a bedroom is cheaper on paper and expensive in the ways that matter. Home office only is here as a comparison, and as a fallback. Rates are based on Melbourne coworking spaces such as T.O.M.S Place in Brunswick.

Professional indemnity covers you when a client claims your work cost them money: a typo that went to print, a missed deadline, a file supplied in the wrong colour space. Expect $500–1,500 a year depending on turnover, and note that most studios and agencies will not engage a contractor who does not hold it. Coworking spaces often require public liability cover as well. Budget for this from your first paid job, not your fiftieth.
