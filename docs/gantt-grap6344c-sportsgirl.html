How to use this & why it matters

A production schedule is how a designer works out whether a job fits the time available before committing to it. Most of the tasks and all of the assessment deadlines are set for you. The first phase is yours to fill in, and the whole timeline is yours to build.

What to do

  1. Fill in your name, student number and anyone you planned with.
  2. Look at the phases below 'Brief, IP & research'. They are mapped out for you. Read a few tasks to see how specific one should be.
  3. Then write the tasks for 'Brief, IP & research' yourself with the + Add a task button, at least three. That phase covers weeks 1 to 4, so you have already done it. Write down what you actually did.
  4. For each task, click a week on the timeline to place its bar, then drag the bar to move it or drag its ends to make it longer or shorter. Double-click a bar to clear it and start again. The length of the bar is how long you think the task will run.
  5. Add an hours estimate for each task. Your total feeds straight into your AT1c costings.
  6. The coloured ◆ diamonds along the top are the assessment deadlines. They're fixed, so use them to check your workload lands before each one.
  7. When the plan check at the bottom is clear, export to Word for your AT1b worksheet.

Your 3c answer describes what happens in each phase. This is where you turn that description into actual tasks.

You can plan this with others, so list who in the "Planned with" field. There's no single right answer: what's assessed is that you understand the sequence, place the work sensibly and estimate effort realistically.
